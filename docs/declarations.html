Export forms

Since 1 October 2026, services exporters that are firms declare each export to their bank on an Export Declaration Form (EDF). The agent works out which invoices need one and when, and prepares the data. You submit it; the agent never files with the bank. The form is the same whichever bank you settle to; what differs is when the bank needs it and whether it treats your service as software or as other services, which the agent asks you to confirm.

Does Saffron Loop Studio LLP need EDFs?

Yes. It is an LLP. On 7 October 2026 the RBI said individuals on personal-nature contracts are not covered; firms still are. The EDF applies to every services invoice regardless of its value. RBI's FAQs are pending (none published as of 12 October), and the agent will update this when they arrive.

When

Within 30 days after the end of the month of the invoice, so October's invoices are due by 30 November 2026. One EDF can cover the whole month. Non-software services may instead be declared on or before the payment is received. Only invoices dated on or after 1 October 2026 are covered.

Why some can't wait

This account settles to ICICI, and while ICICI requires an EDF number before Razorpay captures a MoneySaver bank transfer, three payments already received are blocked until the number exists. An account settling elsewhere files the same form after the money lands.

The regulation, in its own words

RBI Notification FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB, Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026, in force from 1 October 2026, amended on 22 September 2026.

  • Who and when (Regulation 3(2))A services exporter files the EDF "within 30 days from the end of month in which invoice for services has been raised". One EDF may cover all exports in a month (proviso a). A services exporter other than software "may file on or before the date of receipt of payment" instead (proviso b). The bank may extend the deadline on a reasoned request (proviso c).
  • Time to be paid (Regulation 5)Export proceeds are due within nine months. The amendment of 22 September 2026 substituted "nine months" for "fifteen months"; Razorpay's blog of 8 September 2026 still says fifteen, because it predates the amendment. The bank may allow an extension.
  • The ₹10 lakh line (Regulations 4 and 6)For an invoice up to ₹10 lakh the bank may close the export entry, or allow a reduction for a shortfall, "based on a declaration from the exporter". This is about documents and closure. It does not exempt any invoice from the EDF itself.
  • Unpaid for a year past the due date (Regulation 13)The exporter may export only against full advance payment or an irrevocable letter of credit.
  • Individuals (RBI, 7 October 2026)"the individuals are not included with respect to the reporting requirements for the contracts of a personal nature" (Deputy Governor Rohit Jain, via Business Today). FAQs promised; not yet published.
  • ICICI and MoneySaver (Razorpay blog, 21 September 2026)"you will be required to file for EDF number before capturing any payment with Razorpay Moneysaver." Razorpay says it is working with ICICI on a smoother EDF generation process. The agent treats this as one bank's current operating rule, not as the law.

Purpose code check

The account's purpose code: P0802, Software consultancy or implementation (chosen when the account was activated). Five of October's invoices are design work. Sources disagree on the code for design: Razorpay's freelancer guide lists P0806 for graphic design, while the RBI code list Stripe publishes describes P0806 as "Other information services". A wrong code can freeze a remittance, so the agent asks you to confirm with ICICI or your CA rather than guessing.

No EDF needed

SL-1036, SL-1038, SL-1039, SL-1040, SL-1044 and SL-1046 are dated before 1 October 2026. The new rule does not cover them, so Razorpay can capture their payments as soon as they are linked.

Who is responsible for what

  • The agent prepares the data from the invoices and the payments it can see, and tracks the deadlines.
  • You confirm the purpose code and submit the form; the declaration is yours, so its content is your responsibility, not the agent's.
  • The bank reports the export and the realisation in EDPMS; the eBRC is then generated by you on the DGFT portal, self-certified against the bank's record (DGFT, self-certification of eBRC, 2023). Today you type the EDF number in when the bank gives it, which is not bank confirmation; the right loop is an EDF-number field in the Exporter Dashboard that the partner bank confirms.
