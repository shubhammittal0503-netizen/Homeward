How it works

Reading pages: For judges · Test results · Playbook · Learning · Rules-only mode

Homeward is a concept agent for Razorpay's Agent Studio. It works for small Indian services firms paid by overseas clients, and its unit is the invoice, not the payment: an invoice is done only when the money is paid, linked, declared, in the bank and closed.

A normal morning has none to two items; the demo packs twelve into one day.

When things go wrong

SituationWhat the agent does

Confidence below 0.80Asks you, showing its lean and the evidence.
You approved a link to the wrong invoiceBefore capture: unlinks and asks again. After capture: prepares a correction note to Razorpay support and the bank for your approval; demotes the learned payer name; switches auto-approve off until 20 more matches are confirmed.

Where it would live in Razorpay

  • A new agent in Agent Studio, which today has no cross-border agent.
  • Its list shows inside Exporter Dashboard 2.0, which tracks payments but does not act on them.
  • It builds on what exists: MoneySaver global accounts, Payment Links, invoice upload, Apple Pay and Google Pay, and free FIRAs.
  • Morning summaries could reach the owner on WhatsApp through RAY, Razorpay's AI account manager (not built here).
  • Unpaid-invoice reminders stay with the RazorpayX Receivables Agent; disputes stay with Razorpay's dispute tools.
  • RAY already sends a link when a payment fails and answers when asked; Homeward asks why it failed first, and works the list every morning before anyone asks (the full comparison is on For judges).
  • First to ship: the morning list with declines and stuck money for every exporter; the EDF pack first for accounts whose bank needs the number before capture.

Razorpay does not publish its cross-border volume, so this is a transparent model, not a measurement. Every input is sourced or labelled as an assumption; a pilot replaces the assumptions.

For the merchant (base): about 2.6% of overseas income comes back faster and more surely, and ₹2.64 lakh a year stops waiting about 10 days. For Razorpay: the fee on money won back is small (₹924 per exporter); the prize is keeping the exporter, worth about ₹35,200 a year in list fees, in a market where cheaper rivals sell "we don't hold your money". Across 1,000 exporters the base case is about ₹4.6 crore a year won back; each 1% of Razorpay's 100,000 exporters kept is worth about ₹3.5 crore a year. One decision costs Razorpay something on purpose: a declined card rerouted to a bank transfer earns 1% instead of 3%, and the merchant's ceiling is the ceiling; the volume and the exporter stay on Razorpay.

What is real and what is made up

  • Real: the rules and their sources, Razorpay's products and fees, the public merchant quotes, and the blind test method and its results on the published test sets.
  • Made up: Priya, her studio, every client, invoice, email and amount, the learning history, and every step marked "simulated".
  • Not built: connections to Razorpay, banks, Zoho or email; live AI calls (the AI outputs shown were produced with Claude during the build and stored).
  • Not tested: real bank narrations, real email threads, real decline messages; the pilot does that.
